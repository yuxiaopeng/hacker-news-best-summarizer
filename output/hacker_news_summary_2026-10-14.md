## 10. OpenAI工程师因窃听高管会议室被捕

**原文标题**: OpenAI engineer arrested in bugging of executive conference room

**原文链接**: [https://sfstandard.com/2026/10/13/openai-engineer-arrested-alleged-bugging-executive-conference-room/](https://sfstandard.com/2026/10/13/openai-engineer-arrested-alleged-bugging-executive-conference-room/)

OpenAI engineer Abdulrahman Alfozan, 35, a Saudi national, has been arrested and charged with hiding an audio recording device in an executive conference room at the company's San Francisco headquarters. The device, a thumb-sized Seeed XIAO ESP32-S3 Sense, was discovered September 30, concealed in toilet paper and plugged into a television's USB port. Prosecutors allege it was installed around September 28.

Alfozan, who joined OpenAI in July 2025, was not authorized to enter the room, which frequently held discussions about confidential technical, commercial, and strategic information. Surveillance footage placed him near the room, and he had used a productivity tool to inquire about building addresses and meeting locations. A chatbot account linked to his spouse also made queries about building, obscuring, and accessing recording devices, including the specific model found.

Additionally, a second, similar recording device was found in an unattended bag belonging to Alfozan. U.S. Attorney Craig Missakian labeled the incident a "matter of national security," with the FBI emphasizing the protection of U.S. AI innovation as a top counterintelligence priority.

OpenAI discovered the device during a security sweep, utilized its AI models to identify it, and promptly informed the FBI. The company coordinated Alfozan's termination with investigators and stated there is no indication he accessed sensitive data like model weights or source code. If convicted, Alfozan faces up to five years in prison. OpenAI also believes he intended to place more devices in sensitive areas.

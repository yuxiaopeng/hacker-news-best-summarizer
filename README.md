The article advocates for physically cutting "Too Big" books into smaller, more manageable volumes to enhance the reading experience. Citing the 850+ page *Lonesome Dove* as an example, the author argues that oversized books cause hand and arm fatigue, especially when reading in bed, and are inconvenient for travel.

The proposed solution, "Chop That Book Up Into Reasonable Sizes," involves a simple DIY process. First, readers should buy their own copy (not a library book) and identify natural break points like parts, chapter divisions, or binding signatures. Next, carefully "crack" the spine to expose the glue or stitching, then use an X-acto knife to slice the book into sections.

To protect these new, smaller "volumes," the author recommends creating ersatz covers, suggesting manila folders for their firmness and affordability. These folders are folded, trimmed, and glued to the new spine (Elmer's glue works well, secured with binder clips). Finally, the new volumes should be clearly labeled, perhaps with a bold Sharpie. This practical method, the author reassures, is easy, quick, and makes reading large books significantly more comfortable and portable.

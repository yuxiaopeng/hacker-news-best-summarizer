## 8. 私营电力公司收取的电费比公共电力公司高23%。

**原文标题**: Investor-owned utilities charge 23% more for electricity than public providers

**原文链接**: [https://www.kadoa.com/energy-prices/insights/investor-owned-utilities](https://www.kadoa.com/energy-prices/insights/investor-owned-utilities)

2025年，投资者所有的公用事业公司（简称IOU）的电力收费显著更高，平均每千瓦时（kWh）17.8美分，比公共事业公司（14.5美分）高23%，比合作社（14.1美分）高26%。

自2020年以来，IOU电价上涨了34%，而公共事业公司和合作社的电价上涨了19%。这与24%的通货膨胀形成对比，意味着IOU电价实际增长了7.9%，而公共事业公司和合作社的电价在扣除通货膨胀因素后，实际下降了4.3%。这一价格差距的大部分形成于2020年至2023年之间，这与能源价格飙升同时发生。

尽管每千瓦时费率较低，但由于用电量更高，合作社客户在2025年的平均月度电费账单最高（159美元）。公共事业公司受益于免税和能获得更便宜的电力，这有助于降低其成本。

可靠性数据显示，除重大风暴造成的停电外，公共事业公司通常每年停电时间更少（2025年为80分钟），相比之下，IOU为120分钟，合作社为166分钟。

投资者所有的公用事业公司与非营利性供应商之间的价格差距在加利福尼亚州、缅因州和新泽西州等州扩大得最为显著。自2020年以来，包括纽约州电力与燃气公司（+97%）和圣地亚哥燃气与电力公司（+79%）在内的几家大型IOU，电价涨幅尤其剧烈，远超全国通货膨胀率。文章强调，IOU的运营旨在为股东创造回报，而这一因素在公共事业公司和合作社模式中并不存在。

## 9. Your Phone Is About to Stop Being Yours: 70 Days Until Lockdown

**原文标题**: Your Phone Is About to Stop Being Yours: 70 Days Until Lockdown

**原文链接**: [https://keepandroidopen.org/?](https://keepandroidopen.org/?)

Starting in 2027, Google will implement a silent update forcing all Android app developers worldwide to register centrally with the company. This mandate requires paying a fee, agreeing to Google's terms, providing government-issued ID, and surrendering private signing key evidence. Failure to comply will result in apps being silently blocked on every Android device, impacting not just Play Store apps but also those shared between friends, distributed via F-Droid, or built by hobbyists.

This policy is criticized for harming users by retroactively changing the terms of device ownership, independent developers (including open-source projects like F-Droid), and civil society by enabling potential censorship from authoritarian regimes. The described "escape hatch" for power users to sideload apps is deemed a "trap door"—a nine-step, 24-hour wait process managed by Google Play Services, which can be altered or removed at any time.

The "security" rationale is dismissed as a smokescreen, arguing that it's a censorship tool, not a security enhancement, as malware authors can register while dissidents or anonymous developers cannot. Critics highlight that this move dismantles Android's distinguishing open legacy, turning it into a walled garden akin to Apple (which itself is being forced to open up).

A broad coalition of over 70 organizations, including the EFF and FSFE, are urging resistance. Calls to action include installing alternative app stores like F-Droid, contacting regulators, sharing information, signing petitions, and encouraging developers not to comply with Google's registration demands.

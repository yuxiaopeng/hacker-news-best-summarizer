## 8. 我换了执照。

**原文标题**: I changed my license

**原文链接**: [https://bergie.iki.fi/blog/eupl/](https://bergie.iki.fi/blog/eupl/)

The author reflects on 28 years of software licensing, detailing three distinct phases. Initially, their Midgard web framework used LGPLv2, a weak copyleft license. Around 2011, for JavaScript projects, they adopted the permissive MIT license, popular in the NPM ecosystem for its simplicity.

After a period of inactivity, the author has now made the European Union Public License 1.2 (EUPL-1.2) their default. This marks a significant pivot to a strong copyleft license. The author explains this change as a disillusionment with permissive licenses, arguing that the "open source" movement inadvertently benefited corporations and billionaires rather than users or developers. EUPL-1.2 aims to rectify this by closing the "SaaS loophole," mandating reciprocal licensing regardless of how the software is distributed. The author believes corporations unwilling to adhere to these terms should develop their own solutions. An additional benefit of EUPL-1.2 is its 23 official translations, facilitating global use.

Several new projects, including `reticulum-js`, `dacar`, `signalk-energy-predictor`, `offshore-blogging-system`, and the rewritten NoFlo Development Environment, are now under EUPL-1.2. However, the pre-existing NoFlo project will remain MIT-licensed due to its history and third-party contributions.
